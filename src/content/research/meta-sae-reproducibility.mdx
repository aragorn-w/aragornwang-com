## What

A sparse autoencoder (SAE) trained on a language model's activations learns a dictionary of latents. A meta-SAE is a second sparse autoencoder trained on the first one's decoder rows, and its latents are meta-latents. This project asks one question: do meta-latents recur when only the training seed changes?

## How

Every setup is trained with eight seeds. A reference setup on GPT-2 Small is adapted from Leask et al. (2025). The main study covers six meta-SAE widths, from 1/4 to 1/128, on the Gemma Scope 2 SAEs for Gemma 3 270M, 1B, 4B, 12B and 27B.

Each meta-SAE is compared against "noise twin" meta-SAEs, trained on Gaussian rows with the mean and covariance of the parent SAE's decoder rows. The measure is excess agreement: the agreement between seeds minus the noise twins' agreement.

## What it found

In the reference setup, 64.0% of meta-latents recur at a cosine similarity of 0.7 or more, against 0.07% for the noise twins. On Gemma Scope 2, at most 29.9% recur.

By excess agreement, width 1/4 is best for 270M and 1B. At 4B to 27B, every width from 1/32 to 1/128 beats 1/4, with 1.8 to 3.3 times its recurring fraction. Raw agreement peaks at 1/128 for four of the five model sizes, so excess agreement is the better guide for choosing a meta-SAE's width.

## Why

Reproducibility across seeds helps show whether learned latents reflect stable structure in the model or variation between training runs.
