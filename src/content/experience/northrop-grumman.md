Six months at the Autonomous Intelligence and Robotics Lab, working at the intersection of physics-informed ML and computer vision for defense applications. The thesis was that learned models could improve VLF radiowave military communications by predicting ionosphere state more reliably than physics-only methods, and the same lab also needed deployable vision for UAVs and satellite constellations.

- Interned at the Autonomous Intelligence and Robotics Lab (AIR Lab)
- Trained physics-informed graph neural networks and diffusion-based transformers for high-fidelity ionosphere forecasting to improve very low frequency-radiowave military communications
- Generated terabyte-size electron density profiles via multidimensional interpolation for the D and E regions
- Conducted symbolic regression to discover novel ionosphere physics equations
- Developed zero-shot (text to image) semantic segmentation, object detection, and ID assignment neural networks for multiple object tracking robust to deploy onto military UAVs and satellite constellations
- Won _BRAVO to our Stars_ award, twice, for significant engineering, presentation, and white paper contributions
