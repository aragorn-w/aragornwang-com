**Problem.** Ricoh's robotics group needed an object detector deployable on a Universal Robots Cobot arm in a print-shop assembly cell, targeting paper stacks (sheets, books, other printed media) for a pick-and-place loop. The objects of interest, the lighting, and the camera placement were all cell-specific, and real-world labeled data collection wouldn't scale to the iteration cadence we needed.

**Approach.** Architected and built a four-stage synthetic-data pipeline end-to-end on a five-week Mines CSCI 370 summer field session with a four-person team: OpenUSD scene authoring of the cell, Isaac Sim for physically-valid simulation, NVIDIA Cosmos as a diffusion foundation model for photorealistic seeding, and a modified YOLOv11 detector trained on the auto-labeled output. Bounding boxes are carried through from the scene graph rather than generated by a separate labeling pass, so every synthetic frame ships with ground-truth labels for free. Compute on GPU workstations with cloud-burst capacity, environments managed in Docker.

**Outcome.** Pipeline shipped to Ricoh's Robotics Group at handoff: OpenUSD scenes, the Isaac Sim plus Cosmos generator, and a YOLOv11 detector trained on the auto-labeled output. The deeper value is the pipeline itself: each new object class for the cell now costs orders of magnitude less to bring up than a real-world capture campaign would be.
